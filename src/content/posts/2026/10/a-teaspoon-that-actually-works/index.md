I'm full. Properly full. And I still want something.

That's a familiar enough problem by now — stomach saying we're fine, thanks, brain still browsing the menu. What's new is that I think I've found something that actually answers it, rather than just distracting from it.

A teaspoon of crunchy Pip & Nut.

---

It sounds too small to matter, but it hits both things I'm after at once: sweet and savoury in the same mouthful. Roasted, salty, a bit of sugar, properly creamy. A lot of sensory satisfaction packed into a teaspoon.

The bit that actually makes it work, though, is how long it hangs around. A biscuit gives you a hit of sweetness, maybe a bit of crunch, and then it's over before the satisfaction's caught up — which is exactly how you end up reaching for a second one. Peanut butter doesn't do that. It coats your mouth, the flavour develops as you eat it, and the taste lingers well after the spoon's empty. One small mouthful, a genuinely long experience.

---

I checked with myself on this, because it matters which one's actually true: does the teaspoon just distract me for a bit, or does it properly calm the want down?

It calms it. Not forever, not every single time, but for a while the internal conversation actually goes quiet, rather than just getting quieter. That's a real difference. I'm not fighting the urge or negotiating myself out of it. I have the teaspoon, and the wanting settles.

---

I'm not about to turn a teaspoon into a tablespoon just because it tastes good and more would presumably taste better. The amount that works is the amount that works, and there's no reason to improve on something that's already doing its job.

That said, a teaspoon can hold a genuinely surprising amount if you're motivated, and I am motivated. The actual skill here isn't restraint, it's technique: loading as much peanut butter onto the spoon as physically possible without any of it falling off on the way out. There's a rule, and I've decided it's a fair one. It must stay on the spoon. No "oops, dropped a bit" swiped up on a finger. No sly drip off the edge into my mouth on the way out of the jar. That's cheating, and I'll admit I'm tempted to cheat.

Which means the kilo tub I had nowhere to put has finally found a use. At a teaspoon a time, it's also going to last an improbably long while.
