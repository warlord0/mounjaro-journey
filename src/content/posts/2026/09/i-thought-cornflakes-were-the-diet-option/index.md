I'd always assumed cornflakes were the sensible option. Barely any fat, looks light in the bowl, feels like the boring, virtuous breakfast next to two slices of toast dripping in butter.

So I actually did the sum.

---

Two slices of wholemeal toast with butter comes out at somewhere around 250 calories.

A proper bowl of cornflakes, the kind you actually pour rather than the manufacturer's idea of a serving, with half a pint of skimmed milk, comes out at roughly the same. Once you weigh it rather than eyeball it, a "bowl" is very easily 70 or 80 grams of cereal, and the milk alone is adding close to 100 calories on its own.

Same ballpark of calories. Completely different breakfasts.

---

The toast has fibre and a bit of fat in it. The cornflakes are mostly fast carbohydrate and milk, dressed up as something lighter because there's no visible butter involved.

And that's the actual point. Cereal has a bit of a health-food optical illusion going on. It looks insubstantial because there's nothing greasy about it, so your brain files it as the lighter choice without anyone doing the maths.

Calories aren't the same thing as feeling like you've eaten breakfast.

---

Two slices of toast and I'm done. Bowl of cornflakes at the same calorie count, and I'm not sure I'd get the same "that's breakfast sorted" signal from it. Less fibre, less fat, more of the sort of carbohydrate that doesn't hang around asking to be noticed.

Which is exactly the distinction I keep running into. Mounjaro turning the volume down on physical hunger doesn't switch off wanting to eat, and a breakfast that actually feels eaten is worth more to me some mornings than the calorie counter would suggest.

---

I'm not swapping to toast every morning off the back of this. I still like cornflakes, and there are mornings where a big bowl of milk and cereal is exactly what I want anyway.

But I'm not going to keep assuming it's the diet option just because there's no butter in sight. Worth weighing the bowl occasionally, if only to see what "just a bowl of cereal" is actually costing.
