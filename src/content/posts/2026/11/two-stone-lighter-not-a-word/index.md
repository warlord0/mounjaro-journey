Met up with some old work colleagues a while back, probably before Mounjaro. One of them joked that I hadn't changed a bit, still the same size. Harmless banter, didn't bother me in the slightest.

A few months later, we met up again. This time I was two stone lighter.

I said nothing. He said nothing.

---

I told myself I wasn't bothered. The silence said otherwise. I'd been quietly putting in the effort — the eating habits, cutting back the drink, the gastrointestinal adventures nobody asks about, the scales slowly moving the right way — and the one person who'd clocked my size last time didn't clock the change this time. Or did, and didn't say.

We're typical geezers, aren't we. Twenty years of taking the piss out of each other, and asking how someone's actually doing feels like crossing a line nobody's agreed to cross.

---

Here's the bit I didn't expect to admit: if he had said something, I'd have brushed it straight off. "Yeah, a bit." Change the subject. Classic.

So I wanted the acknowledgement, and I almost certainly wouldn't have known what to do with it if it had turned up. Wanted to be noticed, without wanting to be seen wanting it. That's about as far as I've got with untangling it, and I'm not sure it needs untangling any further than that.
