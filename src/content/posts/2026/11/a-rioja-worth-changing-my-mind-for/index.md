My son and his partner had us round for Sunday dinner, and they smashed it out of the park. As close as anything's come to matching the wife's version, which is saying something given the standard she's set. The pork crackling especially was superb.

Mum's roast takes a lot of beating. But I'd rather eat my son's homemade dinner than anything from a fancy restaurant, and that says a fair bit on its own.

But the real surprise was the wine. A Rioja. I have never enjoyed a Spanish wine in my life, and today one actually landed.

---

Shiraz and Merlot have always been safe bets for me. Rioja's been guilty until proven innocent, right alongside French reds — too acidic for my liking, and Italian reds that have always tasted closer to vinegar than wine.

This one was different. Turned out it wasn't just "a Rioja" — it was Aldi's Specially Selected Rioja Reserva, £7.99, 100% Tempranillo. The Reserva bit matters, apparently: at least three years ageing, a year of that in oak, which rounds it out compared to the young, sharper stuff I must have been judging the whole category on.

---

I'm not declaring a new favourite grape on the strength of one bottle and a very good roast dinner. It's entirely possible the pork crackling did some of the heavy lifting. But I'm curious enough to try another Reserva before writing it off as a one-off — turns out I might not dislike Spanish wine after all. I just hadn't found the right bottle yet.
