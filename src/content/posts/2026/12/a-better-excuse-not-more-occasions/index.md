Noticed something about the food. The satisfaction of eating ends well before the evening does. Not hunger — I'm not wanting more dinner. It's that the pleasure of it wears off a few hours before bed, and there's still a stretch of evening left that wants something enjoyable in it.

Used to be a biscuit with tea, a packet of crisps, a wander round the fridge for no good reason. Mounjaro's made all of that far less tempting than it used to be. But it hasn't touched the actual want — the bit of me that wants the evening punctuated with a bit of pleasure. Rum's stepped into that gap instead.

---

Tried testing it properly. Does the craving fade when I'm genuinely absorbed in something — writing, one of the technical projects? Turns out: not really. The rum just lasts longer. Glass sits there, gets sampled rather than finished off, and the two measures stretch out over an evening instead of disappearing in twenty minutes.

Which sounds like progress, except it isn't quite. Taking longer to drink the same amount isn't the same as wanting less of it. All I'd actually found was a way to control the rate, not the total. And the moment the glass runs dry, I notice it — and noticing it is half the pull. The empty glass is doing some of the work here, not just the rum itself.

---

The 35 ml measures are the bit that's actually working. Genuinely less alcohol, not just a slower route to the same amount — provided the number of drinks doesn't creep up to compensate. Smaller measures, poured deliberately, rather than relying on willpower to stop refilling once the taste's in my mouth.

---

Then there's the season. Birthdays, annual leave, Christmas coming, and suddenly a weekday drink doesn't need justifying the way it used to — the calendar itself is the excuse. I'd like to say it's because there genuinely are more occasions worth marking this time of year. Honestly, though, that's not it. The want's the same want I'd have on any ordinary Tuesday. December just makes it easier not to question it.

That's the bit worth remembering going into January — not that the season's indulgence was wrong, but that the permission it hands out doesn't actually expire with the decorations. If I don't notice that, I'll still be running on "well, it's nearly Christmas" logic well into February.
